# Strategy

> **Navigate:** [Home](../../../README.md) · [Catalogue](../../../ROOT_INDEX.md) · [All Knowledge](../README.md) · [Phase 10 Prompt](../../../01_PHASES/PHASE_10_KNOWLEDGE_MASTER_PROMPT.md)

## Executive Summary

Strategy is how an organisation chooses where to play and how to win — the set of integrated choices about markets, customers, value and capabilities that create a durable advantage.

## Purpose

To give people and AI agents a clear, reusable explanation of **Strategy** that supports learning, retrieval (RAG) and decision support.

## Scope

Covers the core ideas, practices and pitfalls of strategy as applied in AIBOS. Deep procedural detail lives in the related playbooks.

## Core Concepts

- Vision, mission and goals translate ambition into direction.
- Where-to-play / how-to-win choices (Playing to Win).
- Competitive advantage and moats (cost, differentiation, network effects).
- OKRs cascade strategy into measurable objectives.
- Strategy must be reviewed as markets and AI capabilities shift.

## Definitions

- **OKR** — Objectives and Key Results — a goal-setting framework.
- **Moat** — A durable competitive advantage.

## Business Context

Understanding strategy helps teams make better decisions, reduce risk and move faster. It connects directly to the related rules, checklists and playbooks below.

## Technical Context

In AIBOS, strategy is applied through the related standards and automated wherever possible, with AI agents operating under human oversight.

## Best Practices

- Make explicit, integrated choices — not a wish list.
- Tie every initiative to a strategic objective and metric.
- Revisit strategy on a cadence; treat it as a hypothesis to test.

## Anti-patterns

- Confusing a plan or budget with a strategy.
- Trying to be everything to everyone.
- Setting strategy once and never revisiting it.

## Design Patterns

Apply the best practices above as repeatable patterns for this topic.

## Examples

- Apply the best practices above to a real strategy task and validate with the related checklist.

## FAQs

**Q: How is strategy different from planning?**

A: Strategy is the set of choices about how to win; planning is how you execute those choices.

**Q: What is a moat?**

A: A structural advantage (e.g. network effects, switching costs) that is hard for competitors to copy.

- Practise framing where-to-play/how-to-win for a real product.
- Connect strategy to OKRs and a quarterly review.
