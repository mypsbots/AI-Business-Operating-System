# Finance

> **Navigate:** [Home](../../../README.md) · [Catalogue](../../../ROOT_INDEX.md) · [All Knowledge](../README.md) · [Phase 10 Prompt](../../../01_PHASES/PHASE_10_KNOWLEDGE_MASTER_PROMPT.md)

## Executive Summary

Business finance is the discipline of planning, allocating and controlling money so the organisation stays solvent, invests wisely and is auditable.

## Purpose

To give people and AI agents a clear, reusable explanation of **Finance** that supports learning, retrieval (RAG) and decision support.

## Scope

Covers the core ideas, practices and pitfalls of finance as applied in AIBOS. Deep procedural detail lives in the related playbooks.

## Core Concepts

- Budgeting and forecasting allocate and predict resources.
- Unit economics (CAC, LTV, margin) reveal whether a business model works.
- Cash flow and runway determine survival.
- Financial controls and segregation of duties prevent fraud and error.
- FinOps applies financial accountability to cloud/AI spend.

## Definitions

- **CAC** — Customer Acquisition Cost.
- **LTV** — Customer Lifetime Value.

## Business Context

Understanding finance helps teams make better decisions, reduce risk and move faster. It connects directly to the related rules, checklists and playbooks below.

## Technical Context

In AIBOS, finance is applied through the related standards and automated wherever possible, with AI agents operating under human oversight.

## Best Practices

- Track unit economics, not just revenue.
- Monitor cash and runway continuously.
- Document forecast assumptions and review variances.

## Anti-patterns

- Ignoring cash flow while chasing growth.
- Untracked cloud/AI spend.
- Opaque, undocumented forecasts.

## Design Patterns

Apply the best practices above as repeatable patterns for this topic.

## Examples

- Apply the best practices above to a real finance task and validate with the related checklist.

## FAQs

**Q: What is runway?**

A: How many months the business can operate at current burn before cash runs out.

**Q: What is LTV:CAC?**

A: Lifetime value relative to customer acquisition cost; >3:1 is a common healthy benchmark.
