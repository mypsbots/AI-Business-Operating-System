## Legal Notice

> **Legal notice.** This skill produces a **draft** partnership document to speed up preparation. It is **not legal advice** and is **not a substitute for review by a UK-qualified solicitor**. Partnership, alliance, reseller, revenue-share and joint-venture arrangements can raise competition-law, tax, employment, agency and unintended-liability issues — obtain solicitor sign-off for exclusive, equity, cross-border, competition-sensitive, high-value or otherwise complex deals before signing. Unless otherwise agreed, the governing law is **England and Wales**. Do not store passwords, API keys or production credentials in generated documents.

## Purpose

A Strategic Partnership (or Alliance) Agreement sets the binding legal and commercial rules for an ongoing collaboration between two independent companies that are not forming a separate legal entity — covering scope, each party's commitments, IP and brand use, economics, governance, liability, confidentiality, term and exit.

It is the backbone under which specific joint activities, co-marketing, reseller/referral arrangements or joint-delivery SOWs are then executed as schedules or linked agreements.

## When to Use

- An ongoing, multi-initiative relationship (not a single bid or one-off deal).
- Where the parties want stable alliance terms with specific activities run under schedules/SOWs.
- As the master agreement that Revenue Share, Co-Marketing, Data Sharing and Governance schedules attach to.

## Document Blueprint (Generation Template)

When this skill is invoked, generate the document by expanding each blueprint section below into clear, formal UK business English, filling every placeholder from the *Inputs Required*, resolving or flagging any missing input, marking the output as a draft, and appending a dated signature block for the authorised signatories. State the binding status explicitly, keep the parties' obligations balanced, and stay consistent with the [UK Partnership Framework](00_UK_PARTNERSHIP_FRAMEWORK.md).

1. **1. Parties and definitions** — Legal names, company numbers, registered offices and defined terms.
2. **2. Purpose and scope of the alliance** — Objectives, fields of collaboration and what is in/out of scope.
3. **3. Nature of relationship** — Independent contractors; no partnership (legal sense), agency, employment or authority to bind.
4. **4. Each party's commitments** — What each party will do, provide and maintain.
5. **5. Exclusivity (if any)** — Scope, territory and duration of any exclusivity (flag for competition-law review).
6. **6. Joint activities and SOWs** — How specific initiatives are agreed and documented (link to Partnership SOW).
7. **7. Economics and revenue sharing** — Charging/revenue/commission model (detail in the Revenue Share Schedule).
8. **8. Intellectual property** — Each keeps Background IP; licences granted; ownership of jointly created IP.
9. **9. Brand and trade-mark use** — Controlled licence for names/logos; approval and quality control (link to Co-Marketing).
10. **10. Confidentiality** — Mutual obligations (or incorporate the Mutual NDA).
11. **11. Data protection** — UK GDPR roles; DPA or Data Sharing Agreement where personal data is involved.
12. **12. Information security** — Security expectations where systems/data are accessed.
13. **13. Warranties** — Authority, skill and care, compliance with law.
14. **14. Anti-bribery and sanctions** — Bribery Act 2010 and sanctions compliance.
15. **15. Compliance with competition law** — Each party complies; no unlawful coordination or information exchange.
16. **16. Liability and indemnities** — Caps, excluded losses, carefully scoped indemnities; non-excludable liabilities preserved.
17. **17. Insurance** — Cover each party maintains.
18. **18. Governance** — Joint Steering Committee, cadence, decisions, escalation (link to Governance ToR).
19. **19. Non-solicitation** — Reasonable, time-limited, where justified.
20. **20. Term and termination** — Duration, renewal, termination for cause/convenience/insolvency/change of control.
21. **21. Consequences of termination and run-off** — Wind-down of live initiatives, trailing economics, licence survival, customer/data handover (link to Exit Deed).
22. **22. Dispute resolution** — Escalation and, optionally, mediation before litigation.
23. **23. Boilerplate** — Notices, assignment, third-party rights, entire agreement, variation, waiver, severability, counterparts, e-signature.
24. **24. Governing law and jurisdiction** — England and Wales; exclusive jurisdiction.
25. **Signatures** — Authorised signatories and dates.

## Inputs Required to Generate

- Both parties' legal details and authorised signatories.
- Alliance scope, each party's commitments and any exclusivity.
- Economic model, IP/brand licences, governance structure.
- Liability caps, insurance levels, term and exit terms.

## UK Legal and Compliance Notes

- Include an explicit 'no legal partnership/agency' clause to avoid Partnership Act 1890 joint liability.
- Exclusivity, pricing coordination and information sharing can engage the Competition Act 1998 — obtain competition-law advice.
- Align the liability cap with available insurance; do not exclude non-excludable liabilities (death/personal injury from negligence, fraud).
- Put the detailed economics in a schedule so the master terms stay stable.

## Best Practices and Pitfalls

- Keep the master agreement stable; run specific initiatives through schedules/SOWs.
- Control brand/trade-mark use with approval and quality rights.
- Have a UK commercial solicitor produce or review the first reusable alliance agreement.
