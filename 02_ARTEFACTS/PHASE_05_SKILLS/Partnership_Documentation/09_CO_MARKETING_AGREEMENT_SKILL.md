## Legal Notice

> **Legal notice.** This skill produces a **draft** partnership document to speed up preparation. It is **not legal advice** and is **not a substitute for review by a UK-qualified solicitor**. Partnership, alliance, reseller, revenue-share and joint-venture arrangements can raise competition-law, tax, employment, agency and unintended-liability issues — obtain solicitor sign-off for exclusive, equity, cross-border, competition-sensitive, high-value or otherwise complex deals before signing. Unless otherwise agreed, the governing law is **England and Wales**. Do not store passwords, API keys or production credentials in generated documents.

## Purpose

A Co-Marketing (and Co-Branding) Agreement lets two companies market together — joint campaigns, content, webinars, events, press and case studies — and use each other's names and logos under a controlled licence, with approval rights, brand guidelines and a fair split of costs and leads.

It protects both brands (quality control, approvals), clarifies who owns jointly created marketing materials and leads, and keeps claims accurate and compliant.

## When to Use

- Joint campaigns, events, webinars, content or PR with a partner.
- Where each party's brand/logo will appear on the other's materials.
- Where leads and marketing costs must be shared fairly.

## Document Blueprint (Generation Template)

When this skill is invoked, generate the document by expanding each blueprint section below into clear, formal UK business English, filling every placeholder from the *Inputs Required*, resolving or flagging any missing input, marking the output as a draft, and appending a dated signature block for the authorised signatories. State the binding status explicitly, keep the parties' obligations balanced, and stay consistent with the [UK Partnership Framework](00_UK_PARTNERSHIP_FRAMEWORK.md).

1. **Parties and campaign scope** — The joint marketing activities and the period covered.
2. **Brand and trade-mark licence** — Limited, revocable licence to use names/logos for the agreed activities only.
3. **Brand guidelines and approvals** — Each party approves uses of its brand before publication; quality control.
4. **Content ownership and licences** — Who owns jointly created materials; licences to reuse.
5. **Messaging and claims** — Accurate, substantiated claims; compliance with advertising rules.
6. **Leads and data** — How jointly generated leads are shared; UK GDPR basis and transparency (link to Data Sharing).
7. **Costs and contributions** — Cost split and each party's contributions.
8. **Responsibilities and timeline** — Who delivers what, by when.
9. **Confidentiality** — Pre-announcement confidentiality; embargoes.
10. **Term and termination** — Duration; takedown of brand use on termination.
11. **Relationship clause** — No partnership (legal sense) or authority to bind.
12. **Governing law** — England and Wales.
13. **Signatures** — Authorised signatories and dates.

## Inputs Required to Generate

- The campaign/activities, period and each party's contributions.
- Brand-use rules and approval process.
- Lead-sharing and data-protection basis; cost split.
- Ownership of co-created content.

## UK Legal and Compliance Notes

- Trade-mark use should be controlled (approval + quality control); uncontrolled licensing can harm the mark.
- Marketing claims must be accurate and substantiated (ASA/CAP Code); correct each other's misstatements.
- Lead lists are personal data — share only with a lawful basis and transparency under UK GDPR.

## Best Practices and Pitfalls

- Agree brand approval SLAs so campaigns are not blocked.
- Decide content ownership and reuse rights before producing assets.
- Require prompt takedown of brand use after the campaign ends.
