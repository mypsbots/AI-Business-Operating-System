## Legal Notice

> **Legal notice.** This skill produces a **draft** partnership document to speed up preparation. It is **not legal advice** and is **not a substitute for review by a UK-qualified solicitor**. Partnership, alliance, reseller, revenue-share and joint-venture arrangements can raise competition-law, tax, employment, agency and unintended-liability issues — obtain solicitor sign-off for exclusive, equity, cross-border, competition-sensitive, high-value or otherwise complex deals before signing. Unless otherwise agreed, the governing law is **England and Wales**. Do not store passwords, API keys or production credentials in generated documents.

## Purpose

A Teaming Agreement commits two (or more) companies to collaborate on a specific bid, tender or opportunity — defining who leads, each party's role and work-share, bid responsibilities and costs, exclusivity for that opportunity, and the agreement to enter a subcontract or joint contract if the bid succeeds.

It de-risks joint pursuits by fixing the ground rules before the win, so the parties are not negotiating basic terms under award-deadline pressure.

## When to Use

- Pursuing a single client tender, framework or RFP jointly.
- Where one party is prime/lead and the other is a specialist partner.
- Where exclusivity and a defined work-share for that opportunity are needed up front.

## Document Blueprint (Generation Template)

When this skill is invoked, generate the document by expanding each blueprint section below into clear, formal UK business English, filling every placeholder from the *Inputs Required*, resolving or flagging any missing input, marking the output as a draft, and appending a dated signature block for the authorised signatories. State the binding status explicitly, keep the parties' obligations balanced, and stay consistent with the [UK Partnership Framework](00_UK_PARTNERSHIP_FRAMEWORK.md).

1. **Parties and roles** — Identify the lead/prime and the partner, and the opportunity being pursued.
2. **The opportunity** — The specific client, tender or RFP, and its scope.
3. **Exclusivity for the opportunity** — Neither party teams with a competitor for this opportunity during the bid.
4. **Bid responsibilities** — Who prepares what; timelines; approvals; single point of contact with the client.
5. **Work-share on award** — Each party's intended scope/share of the work if the bid wins.
6. **Bid costs** — Each party bears its own bid costs unless agreed otherwise.
7. **Confidentiality** — Protection of each party's and the client's information (or reference the NDA).
8. **Commitment to contract on award** — Obligation to negotiate in good faith and sign a subcontract or joint contract if awarded (link to Partnership SOW/subcontract).
9. **Intellectual property** — Ownership of bid materials and each party's Background IP.
10. **Publicity** — No announcements without consent and (where required) the client's consent.
11. **Term** — Runs until award, non-award, or withdrawal of the opportunity.
12. **Relationship clause** — Independent parties; no partnership (legal sense) or authority to bind beyond the bid.
13. **Governing law** — England and Wales.
14. **Signatures** — Authorised signatories and dates.

## Inputs Required to Generate

- The specific opportunity (client, tender reference, scope).
- Lead/prime and partner roles; intended work-share on award.
- Bid responsibilities, timeline and cost treatment.
- Exclusivity scope and duration for the opportunity.

## UK Legal and Compliance Notes

- Keep exclusivity limited to the specific opportunity to reduce competition-law risk; broad exclusivity needs review under the Competition Act 1998.
- Make the 'commit to contract if awarded' obligation clear but realistic — an unconditional pre-agreed subcontract is cleaner than an 'agreement to agree'.
- Respect the client's own procurement rules on teaming, subcontracting and disclosure.

## Best Practices and Pitfalls

- Attach or pre-agree the key subcontract terms so award does not trigger a fresh negotiation.
- Define a single client-facing point of contact to avoid mixed messages.
- State clearly who owns the bid materials.
