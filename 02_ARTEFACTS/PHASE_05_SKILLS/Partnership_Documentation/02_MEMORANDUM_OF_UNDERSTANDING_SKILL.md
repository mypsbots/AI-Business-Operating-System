## Legal Notice

> **Legal notice.** This skill produces a **draft** partnership document to speed up preparation. It is **not legal advice** and is **not a substitute for review by a UK-qualified solicitor**. Partnership, alliance, reseller, revenue-share and joint-venture arrangements can raise competition-law, tax, employment, agency and unintended-liability issues — obtain solicitor sign-off for exclusive, equity, cross-border, competition-sensitive, high-value or otherwise complex deals before signing. Unless otherwise agreed, the governing law is **England and Wales**. Do not store passwords, API keys or production credentials in generated documents.

## Purpose

A Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) records a shared understanding between two organisations about why and how they intend to collaborate — objectives, respective roles, contributions, and the areas they will work on — without (usually) creating binding commercial obligations.

It is widely used to signal commitment publicly or internally, to frame a longer-term relationship, and to set the ground rules before (or alongside) binding agreements such as an Alliance Agreement or specific SOWs.

## When to Use

- To frame a strategic relationship or collaboration programme at a high level.
- Where the parties want a documented understanding but are not ready for binding commitments.
- For public-sector, academic, not-for-profit or ecosystem collaborations where an MOU is the expected instrument.

## Document Blueprint (Generation Template)

When this skill is invoked, generate the document by expanding each blueprint section below into clear, formal UK business English, filling every placeholder from the *Inputs Required*, resolving or flagging any missing input, marking the output as a draft, and appending a dated signature block for the authorised signatories. State the binding status explicitly, keep the parties' obligations balanced, and stay consistent with the [UK Partnership Framework](00_UK_PARTNERSHIP_FRAMEWORK.md).

1. **Parties and background** — Legal names and the context/motivation for collaborating.
2. **Shared objectives** — The outcomes both organisations want from the collaboration.
3. **Scope of collaboration** — Areas, activities and initiatives the parties will pursue together.
4. **Roles and contributions** — What each party will contribute (people, technology, access, funding, brand).
5. **Governance and contacts** — Named relationship owners, meeting cadence, how decisions are coordinated.
6. **Resourcing and costs** — Each party bears its own costs unless a separate agreement says otherwise.
7. **Intellectual property ground rules** — Each keeps its Background IP; separate agreements govern any jointly created IP.
8. **Confidentiality** — Binding confidentiality or reference to the Mutual NDA.
9. **Data protection** — Personal data shared only under a separate DPA/Data Sharing Agreement.
10. **Status — non-binding** — Express statement that the MOU does not create legally binding obligations except the stated clauses.
11. **Term and review** — Duration, review points and how either party may withdraw.
12. **Relationship clause** — No partnership (legal sense), agency or authority to bind the other.
13. **Governing law** — England and Wales.
14. **Signatures** — Authorised signatories and dates.

## Inputs Required to Generate

- Both organisations' legal details and relationship owners.
- Shared objectives, scope of collaboration and each party's contributions.
- Governance/meeting cadence; review and withdrawal terms.
- Which clauses (if any) are intended to bind.

## UK Legal and Compliance Notes

- State unambiguously that the MOU is not legally binding (except named clauses); courts look at substance, so avoid definite obligation language elsewhere.
- Include the 'no legal partnership/agency' clause so the MOU cannot imply a Partnership Act 1890 relationship.
- If personal data will be shared, do not rely on the MOU — put a Data Sharing Agreement or DPA in place.

## Best Practices and Pitfalls

- Keep it strategic and short; move binding detail into the Alliance Agreement or an SOW.
- Name a relationship owner on each side so the MOU is acted on, not shelved.
- Set a review date and a clean, no-fault withdrawal mechanism.
