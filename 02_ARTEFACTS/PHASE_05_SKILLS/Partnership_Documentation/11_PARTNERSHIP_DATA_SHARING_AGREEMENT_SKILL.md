## Legal Notice

> **Legal notice.** This skill produces a **draft** partnership document to speed up preparation. It is **not legal advice** and is **not a substitute for review by a UK-qualified solicitor**. Partnership, alliance, reseller, revenue-share and joint-venture arrangements can raise competition-law, tax, employment, agency and unintended-liability issues — obtain solicitor sign-off for exclusive, equity, cross-border, competition-sensitive, high-value or otherwise complex deals before signing. Unless otherwise agreed, the governing law is **England and Wales**. Do not store passwords, API keys or production credentials in generated documents.

## Purpose

A Partnership Data Sharing Agreement governs the sharing of personal data between partners who each decide their own purposes for the data (controller-to-controller) — for example exchanging leads, contacts or joint-customer information in an alliance, reseller or referral relationship.

It records the purpose and lawful basis for sharing, the data involved, security, data-subject rights, retention and liability, in line with the ICO Data Sharing Code. Where instead one partner processes data on the other's behalf, a Data Processing Agreement is the correct instrument.

## When to Use

- Partners exchange leads, contacts or customer data for their own purposes.
- Independent or joint controller relationships in a partnership.
- Any partnership personal-data sharing not covered by a processor DPA.

## Document Blueprint (Generation Template)

When this skill is invoked, generate the document by expanding each blueprint section below into clear, formal UK business English, filling every placeholder from the *Inputs Required*, resolving or flagging any missing input, marking the output as a draft, and appending a dated signature block for the authorised signatories. State the binding status explicitly, keep the parties' obligations balanced, and stay consistent with the [UK Partnership Framework](00_UK_PARTNERSHIP_FRAMEWORK.md).

1. **Parties and roles** — Each party as controller; independent vs joint controllers.
2. **Purpose of sharing** — Why data is shared and the lawful basis for each party (e.g. legitimate interests, consent).
3. **Data shared** — Categories of personal data and data subjects (e.g. lead contact details).
4. **Method and frequency** — How and how often data is shared; format and security in transit.
5. **Transparency to data subjects** — How individuals are told their data is shared (privacy information).
6. **Security measures** — Protection in transit and at rest; access controls.
7. **Data-subject rights** — How access/erasure/objection requests are handled across both parties.
8. **Joint-controller arrangement** — Where joint controllers, the allocation of responsibilities and a contact point.
9. **Retention and deletion** — How long each party keeps the data; deletion on exit.
10. **International transfers** — Mechanism where data leaves the UK (IDTA / UK Addendum).
11. **Breach handling** — Notification timelines and cooperation.
12. **Liability and indemnity** — Allocation between the controllers.
13. **Governing law** — England and Wales.
14. **Signatures** — Authorised signatories and dates.

## Inputs Required to Generate

- Purpose, lawful basis and data categories/subjects.
- Sharing method, frequency and security.
- Retention, transfers and breach-handling terms.
- Whether the parties are independent or joint controllers.

## UK Legal and Compliance Notes

- Use this (not a processor DPA) when both parties determine their own purposes; follow the ICO Data Sharing Code.
- Confirm a lawful basis and transparency before any lead/contact data is shared — consent or legitimate interests, documented.
- For joint controllers, UK GDPR requires a clear allocation of responsibilities and a data-subject contact point.

## Best Practices and Pitfalls

- Be explicit about who is controller for what, and the lawful basis each relies on.
- Minimise shared data to what the partnership actually needs.
- Keep a record of the sharing decision and privacy information provided to individuals.
