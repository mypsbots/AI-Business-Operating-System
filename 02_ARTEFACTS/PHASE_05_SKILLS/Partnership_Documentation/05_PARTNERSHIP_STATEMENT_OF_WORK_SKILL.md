## Legal Notice

> **Legal notice.** This skill produces a **draft** partnership document to speed up preparation. It is **not legal advice** and is **not a substitute for review by a UK-qualified solicitor**. Partnership, alliance, reseller, revenue-share and joint-venture arrangements can raise competition-law, tax, employment, agency and unintended-liability issues — obtain solicitor sign-off for exclusive, equity, cross-border, competition-sensitive, high-value or otherwise complex deals before signing. Unless otherwise agreed, the governing law is **England and Wales**. Do not store passwords, API keys or production credentials in generated documents.

## Purpose

A Partnership Statement of Work (SOW) describes a single joint engagement delivered by both partners: the combined scope and deliverables, the split of responsibilities between the parties, the timeline, the economics (revenue split and who invoices the client), and objective acceptance criteria.

It differs from a client-delivery SOW by making the inter-party division of work and revenue explicit, so both partners know exactly who does what, who carries which risk, and how money flows.

## When to Use

- Delivering a specific project jointly under an Alliance or Teaming Agreement.
- Where responsibilities and revenue must be split clearly between the partners.
- Where the end client needs one combined scope and acceptance.

## Document Blueprint (Generation Template)

When this skill is invoked, generate the document by expanding each blueprint section below into clear, formal UK business English, filling every placeholder from the *Inputs Required*, resolving or flagging any missing input, marking the output as a draft, and appending a dated signature block for the authorised signatories. State the binding status explicitly, keep the parties' obligations balanced, and stay consistent with the [UK Partnership Framework](00_UK_PARTNERSHIP_FRAMEWORK.md).

1. **SOW reference and governing agreement** — Unique number; effective date; the Alliance/Teaming Agreement it runs under.
2. **Engagement background and objectives** — The client/opportunity and the outcomes sought.
3. **Combined scope and deliverables** — The full scope and each tangible output.
4. **Split of responsibilities** — Exactly which party delivers which workstream/deliverable.
5. **Dependencies between partners** — What each partner must provide to the other and by when.
6. **Timeline and milestones** — Phased plan with owners across both parties.
7. **Client-facing and lead party** — Who contracts with and invoices the client; who leads delivery.
8. **Economics and revenue split** — Fees, the split between partners, and payment flow (link to Revenue Share Schedule).
9. **Acceptance criteria** — Objective, measurable criteria per deliverable and the acceptance process.
10. **Intellectual property** — Ownership/licence of deliverables and each party's Background IP.
11. **Data protection and security** — Flag where personal data is processed (trigger DPA/Data Sharing Agreement).
12. **Liability between partners** — How risk and liability are shared for this engagement.
13. **Signatures** — Authorised signatories and dates.

## Inputs Required to Generate

- Governing Alliance/Teaming Agreement reference; SOW number and dates.
- Combined scope, deliverables and the split of responsibilities between partners.
- Economics and revenue split; who invoices the client.
- Acceptance criteria; IP, data-protection and security flags.

## UK Legal and Compliance Notes

- Make acceptance criteria objective and testable, as with a client SOW (see the Business Docs Statement of Work).
- Be explicit about which partner carries delivery and payment risk, and how shortfalls are handled.
- Where personal data is processed jointly, put the correct UK GDPR instrument in place before starting.

## Best Practices and Pitfalls

- Map every deliverable to exactly one responsible partner.
- Tie the revenue split to the responsibility split to avoid disputes.
- Keep the client-facing scope consistent with each partner's internal scope.
