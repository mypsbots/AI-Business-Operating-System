## Legal Notice

> **Legal notice.** This skill produces a **draft** partnership document to speed up preparation. It is **not legal advice** and is **not a substitute for review by a UK-qualified solicitor**. Partnership, alliance, reseller, revenue-share and joint-venture arrangements can raise competition-law, tax, employment, agency and unintended-liability issues — obtain solicitor sign-off for exclusive, equity, cross-border, competition-sensitive, high-value or otherwise complex deals before signing. Unless otherwise agreed, the governing law is **England and Wales**. Do not store passwords, API keys or production credentials in generated documents.

## Purpose

A Partnership Exit (or Disengagement) Deed records how a partnership ends cleanly — the effective exit date, run-off of existing deals and trailing commissions, transition of joint customers, return or deletion of data, treatment of jointly created IP and brand use, surviving obligations, and any mutual releases.

It prevents a messy or disputed break-up by agreeing, in advance or at exit, exactly what each party keeps, owes and must do, protecting customers and both reputations.

## When to Use

- Ending an alliance, reseller, referral or JV relationship.
- On expiry, termination, or a change of strategy/control.
- Whenever customers, data, IP or trailing money must be disentangled.

## Document Blueprint (Generation Template)

When this skill is invoked, generate the document by expanding each blueprint section below into clear, formal UK business English, filling every placeholder from the *Inputs Required*, resolving or flagging any missing input, marking the output as a draft, and appending a dated signature block for the authorised signatories. State the binding status explicitly, keep the parties' obligations balanced, and stay consistent with the [UK Partnership Framework](00_UK_PARTNERSHIP_FRAMEWORK.md).

1. **Parties and the ending agreement** — Identify the partnership agreement being wound down and the exit date.
2. **Reason and effective date** — Basis for exit (expiry, termination, mutual) and when it takes effect.
3. **Run-off of existing deals** — How in-flight deals/customers are completed or transferred.
4. **Trailing economics** — Commissions/revenue share still payable, and for how long.
5. **Customer transition** — How joint/channel customers are transitioned and communicated to.
6. **Data return and deletion** — Return or secure deletion of personal and confidential data (subject to legal retention).
7. **Intellectual property and brand** — Ceasing brand/trade-mark use; treatment of jointly created IP; licence survival/termination.
8. **Outstanding liabilities** — Settlement of amounts owed each way.
9. **Surviving obligations** — Confidentiality, data protection, non-solicit and other terms that survive.
10. **Mutual releases** — Where agreed, releases of claims (take legal advice).
11. **Announcements** — Agreed messaging about the end of the partnership.
12. **Governing law** — England and Wales.
13. **Execution as a deed** — Signatures/witnessing as required for a deed.

## Inputs Required to Generate

- The agreement being wound down and the exit date.
- Run-off, trailing-commission and customer-transition plan.
- Data return/deletion and IP/brand treatment.
- Any mutual releases and agreed announcements.

## UK Legal and Compliance Notes

- Where the parties give mutual releases or other promises without consideration, execute as a deed (with correct witnessing).
- Honour transitional protection for live customers so they are not stranded on exit.
- Confirm data-return/deletion obligations are met and documented for UK GDPR accountability.

## Best Practices and Pitfalls

- Agree exit mechanics early (in the main agreement) so this deed is quick to produce.
- Protect customers first — a clean handover protects both reputations.
- Document that data and brand use have actually ceased.
