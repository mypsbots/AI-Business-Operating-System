## Legal Notice

> **Legal notice.** This skill produces a **draft** partnership document to speed up preparation. It is **not legal advice** and is **not a substitute for review by a UK-qualified solicitor**. Partnership, alliance, reseller, revenue-share and joint-venture arrangements can raise competition-law, tax, employment, agency and unintended-liability issues — obtain solicitor sign-off for exclusive, equity, cross-border, competition-sensitive, high-value or otherwise complex deals before signing. Unless otherwise agreed, the governing law is **England and Wales**. Do not store passwords, API keys or production credentials in generated documents.

## Purpose

A Letter of Intent (LOI) or Heads of Terms sets out, early and quickly, that two companies intend to work towards a partnership and the principal terms they have provisionally agreed — scope, structure, economics and timeline — so negotiation of the full agreement starts from a shared position.

It is normally non-binding as to the commercial deal, with a small number of clauses (confidentiality, exclusivity, costs, governing law) deliberately made binding. Its status must be stated expressly to avoid accidentally creating a contract.

## When to Use

- At the start of a potential partnership, alliance, reseller, JV or acquisition-style discussion.
- To secure a period of exclusivity while the full agreement is negotiated.
- To align both sides' decision-makers on headline terms before investing in legal drafting.

## Document Blueprint (Generation Template)

When this skill is invoked, generate the document by expanding each blueprint section below into clear, formal UK business English, filling every placeholder from the *Inputs Required*, resolving or flagging any missing input, marking the output as a draft, and appending a dated signature block for the authorised signatories. State the binding status explicitly, keep the parties' obligations balanced, and stay consistent with the [UK Partnership Framework](00_UK_PARTNERSHIP_FRAMEWORK.md).

1. **Parties and purpose** — Legal names, company numbers and the purpose of the proposed collaboration.
2. **Summary of the proposed deal** — High-level description of the partnership model and what each party brings.
3. **Key proposed terms** — Provisional scope, structure, economics (margin/commission/share), term and territory.
4. **Conditions to a binding deal** — What must happen first (due diligence, approvals, definitive agreement).
5. **Exclusivity** — Whether and for how long the parties will not negotiate with others (binding, if agreed).
6. **Confidentiality** — Binding confidentiality (or reference to the existing Mutual NDA).
7. **Costs** — Each party bears its own costs unless agreed otherwise.
8. **Binding vs non-binding statement** — Explicitly state which clauses bind and that the rest is subject to contract.
9. **Timetable** — Indicative milestones towards signing the definitive agreement.
10. **Governing law and jurisdiction** — England and Wales (binding).
11. **Signatures** — Authorised signatories and dates.

## Inputs Required to Generate

- Names, company numbers and registered addresses of both parties.
- Headline deal terms (model, scope, economics, term, territory).
- Whether exclusivity applies and for how long; which clauses are intended to bind.
- Target timetable to a definitive agreement.

## UK Legal and Compliance Notes

- Mark the document 'subject to contract' and state clearly which clauses are binding — otherwise sufficiently certain terms acted upon can form a contract.
- Keep deal language provisional ('the parties intend to'), not definite ('the parties shall'), for non-binding sections.
- Exclusivity, confidentiality, costs and governing law are the clauses usually made binding.

## Best Practices and Pitfalls

- Reference the signed Mutual NDA rather than restating full confidentiality.
- Do not include firm prices or fixed delivery promises you are not ready to be held to.
- Set a realistic deadline so the LOI does not drift.
