## Legal Notice

> **Legal notice.** This skill produces a **draft** partnership document to speed up preparation. It is **not legal advice** and is **not a substitute for review by a UK-qualified solicitor**. Partnership, alliance, reseller, revenue-share and joint-venture arrangements can raise competition-law, tax, employment, agency and unintended-liability issues — obtain solicitor sign-off for exclusive, equity, cross-border, competition-sensitive, high-value or otherwise complex deals before signing. Unless otherwise agreed, the governing law is **England and Wales**. Do not store passwords, API keys or production credentials in generated documents.

## Purpose

A Joint Venture (JV) Agreement sets up a deeper collaboration where the parties pool resources to pursue an opportunity together — either by forming a new jointly owned company (corporate JV) or by a purely contractual JV — governing ownership/shareholding, capital and funding, governance and board, IP contributions, profit share, deadlock and exit.

Because a JV can create shared ownership, shared liabilities and significant tax and competition consequences, it is the heaviest partnership instrument and must be structured with professional advice.

## When to Use

- The parties will share ownership, risk and reward in a venture (not just collaborate).
- Forming a new jointly owned company, or a long-term contractual JV.
- Where capital contribution, profit share and governance of a shared venture are needed.

## Document Blueprint (Generation Template)

When this skill is invoked, generate the document by expanding each blueprint section below into clear, formal UK business English, filling every placeholder from the *Inputs Required*, resolving or flagging any missing input, marking the output as a draft, and appending a dated signature block for the authorised signatories. State the binding status explicitly, keep the parties' obligations balanced, and stay consistent with the [UK Partnership Framework](00_UK_PARTNERSHIP_FRAMEWORK.md).

1. **Parties and JV structure** — Corporate (new company) or contractual JV; the venture's purpose and scope.
2. **Ownership / shareholding** — Each party's stake; classes of shares; the new entity's details (if incorporated).
3. **Capital and funding** — Initial contributions; further funding; what happens if a party cannot fund.
4. **Contributions in kind** — IP, technology, people, customers, brand each party contributes, and on what terms.
5. **Governance and board** — Board composition, appointment rights, quorum, chair, meeting cadence.
6. **Reserved matters** — Decisions needing special/unanimous approval (budgets, borrowing, new business, IP).
7. **Profit share and distributions** — How profits are shared and distributed.
8. **Intellectual property** — Ownership/licensing of contributed and JV-created IP.
9. **Competition-law compliance** — No unlawful coordination; information controls between the venturers.
10. **Deadlock resolution** — Mechanism for resolving board/shareholder deadlock.
11. **Transfer of interests** — Restrictions on share transfer; pre-emption; change of control.
12. **Term and exit** — Duration; exit routes; buy-out (put/call); wind-up and asset distribution (link to Exit Deed).
13. **Warranties, liability and indemnities** — Between the venturers and to/from the JV.
14. **Confidentiality and non-compete** — Protecting the venture; reasonable restrictive covenants.
15. **Governing law and jurisdiction** — England and Wales.
16. **Signatures** — Authorised signatories and dates.

## Inputs Required to Generate

- JV purpose and structure (corporate vs contractual).
- Ownership split, capital contributions and funding plan.
- Governance, reserved matters and profit-share terms.
- IP contributions; exit, deadlock and transfer terms.

## UK Legal and Compliance Notes

- A corporate JV also needs constitutional documents (articles, shareholders' agreement) and Companies House filings — this agreement should align with them.
- JVs can raise merger-control and competition-law issues; obtain competition advice, especially between competitors.
- Tax structuring (corporation tax, VAT, profit extraction) is material — obtain tax advice before signing.
- This is the highest-risk instrument here — do not sign without a solicitor.

## Best Practices and Pitfalls

- Decide deadlock and exit mechanics before signing, not after a dispute.
- Keep reserved matters tight so neither party is trapped by the other.
- Separate contributed Background IP from JV-created IP clearly.
