## Legal Notice

> **Legal notice.** This skill produces a **draft** business document to speed up preparation. It is **not legal advice** and is **not a substitute for review by a UK-qualified solicitor**. For high-value, regulated, international, security-sensitive or complex engagements, obtain solicitor sign-off before signing. Unless otherwise agreed, the governing law is **England and Wales**. Do not store passwords, API keys or production credentials in generated documents.

## Purpose

A Mutual NDA lets the Supplier (e.g. Lemuran Ltd) and a prospective customer exchange confidential information safely during discovery, scoping and due diligence, before any Master Services Agreement or Statement of Work is in place.

It protects secrecy only; it does not authorise any work or transfer any intellectual property, and it is not a substitute for the main services contract.

## When to Use

- Discussing a customer's internal processes, systems or roadmap.
- Reviewing source code, credentials, datasets, financial information, customer records or architecture.
- Presenting the Supplier's confidential methods, reusable assets, pricing logic or product roadmap.
- Engaging a subcontractor or implementation partner who will see confidential information.

## Document Blueprint (Generation Template)

When this skill is invoked, generate the document by expanding each blueprint section below into clear, formal UK business English, filling every placeholder from the *Inputs Required*, resolving or flagging any missing input, marking the output as a draft, and appending a dated signature block for the authorised signatories. Keep numbering and the order of precedence consistent with the [UK Contracting Framework](00_UK_CONTRACTING_FRAMEWORK.md).

1. **Parties** — Full legal names, company numbers and registered addresses of both parties; effective date.
2. **Purpose / permitted use** — The specific purpose for which information is shared; use limited strictly to that purpose.
3. **Definition of Confidential Information** — Broad definition covering written, oral, visual and electronic information, and information about the discussions themselves.
4. **Exclusions** — Information that is public, already known, independently developed, or lawfully received from a third party.
5. **Persons permitted to receive** — Named roles / affiliates / advisers on a need-to-know basis, bound by equivalent obligations.
6. **Security obligations** — Reasonable technical and organisational measures to protect the information.
7. **Legally required disclosure** — Carve-out allowing disclosure required by law/regulator, with notice where lawful.
8. **No IP transfer / no licence** — Nothing transfers ownership or grants any licence beyond the stated purpose.
9. **Return or deletion** — On request or termination, return or securely delete confidential information (subject to legal retention).
10. **Duration of confidentiality** — Confidentiality survives for a stated period after termination (e.g. 3–5 years; trade secrets indefinitely).
11. **Remedies for breach** — Acknowledgement that damages may be inadequate and injunctive relief may be sought.
12. **General / boilerplate** — No partnership, entire agreement, variation in writing, no waiver, severability, counterparts.
13. **Governing law and jurisdiction** — England and Wales; courts of England and Wales have exclusive jurisdiction.
14. **Signature block** — Name, title, signature and date for each authorised signatory.

## Inputs Required to Generate

- Supplier and Customer legal names, company numbers, registered addresses.
- Effective date and the specific purpose of disclosure.
- Confidentiality duration (default 3 years; longer for trade secrets).
- Named recipients / affiliates / advisers permitted to receive information.

## UK Legal and Compliance Notes

- Mutual (two-way) form is standard when both sides disclose; use a one-way NDA only if disclosure is genuinely one-directional.
- Do not over-claim — a court will not protect genuinely public information.
- An NDA does not replace data-protection terms; if personal data is shared to be processed, a DPA is also required.

## Best Practices and Pitfalls

- Sign the NDA before detailed disclosure, not after.
- Keep the permitted purpose narrow and specific.
- Avoid perpetual blanket confidentiality except for genuine trade secrets.
