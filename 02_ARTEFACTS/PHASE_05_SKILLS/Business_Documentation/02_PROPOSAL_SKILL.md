## Legal Notice

> **Legal notice.** This skill produces a **draft** business document to speed up preparation. It is **not legal advice** and is **not a substitute for review by a UK-qualified solicitor**. For high-value, regulated, international, security-sensitive or complex engagements, obtain solicitor sign-off before signing. Unless otherwise agreed, the governing law is **England and Wales**. Do not store passwords, API keys or production credentials in generated documents.

## Purpose

A Proposal sets out what the Supplier recommends and the commercial terms of the offer, converting discovery into a clear solution, scope and price the customer can accept.

It must be marked to control its legal status: 'Subject to contract' when not intended to bind, or 'Accepted as part of the Agreement' when it will form part of the signed contract.

## When to Use

- After a discovery call, to present a recommended solution and price.
- When the customer needs assumptions, exclusions and dependencies stated before committing.
- As the commercial narrative that the MSA and SOW then formalise.

## Document Blueprint (Generation Template)

When this skill is invoked, generate the document by expanding each blueprint section below into clear, formal UK business English, filling every placeholder from the *Inputs Required*, resolving or flagging any missing input, marking the output as a draft, and appending a dated signature block for the authorised signatories. Keep numbering and the order of precedence consistent with the [UK Contracting Framework](00_UK_CONTRACTING_FRAMEWORK.md).

1. **Title and status marking** — Clearly mark 'Subject to contract' or 'Accepted as part of the Agreement'; add version and date.
2. **Customer problem and desired outcome** — The problem to solve and the outcome the customer wants.
3. **Proposed solution** — The recommended approach, technology and how it meets the outcome.
4. **Scope summary** — What is included, at a high level.
5. **Deliverables** — Tangible outputs the customer will receive.
6. **Assumptions** — The basis of the estimate; changes to assumptions may change price/timeline.
7. **Exclusions** — What is explicitly not included.
8. **Expected timeline** — Indicative phases and duration, subject to a signed SOW.
9. **Fees and VAT position** — Fees, whether VAT applies, and expenses treatment.
10. **Payment schedule** — Deposit, milestones and final balance.
11. **Customer dependencies** — What the customer must provide (access, data, decisions).
12. **Optional services** — Add-ons the customer may select.
13. **Validity period** — How long the offer stands (e.g. 30 days).
14. **Reference to contract terms** — Link/reference to the MSA/SOW that will govern the work.
15. **Commencement statement** — Work begins only when the Agreement is signed and any required deposit is paid.

## Inputs Required to Generate

- Discovery findings: problem, outcome, constraints.
- Recommended solution, scope, deliverables, assumptions, exclusions.
- Fees, VAT position, payment schedule, validity period.
- Customer dependencies and any optional services.

## UK Legal and Compliance Notes

- A proposal with sufficiently definite terms can form a binding contract if accepted — always mark its status.
- Keep assumptions and exclusions explicit to control scope and price.

## Best Practices and Pitfalls

- Always state a validity period and the 'work starts on signature + deposit' rule.
- Mirror the Proposal's scope into the SOW to avoid inconsistency.
- Reference (do not contradict) the MSA terms.
